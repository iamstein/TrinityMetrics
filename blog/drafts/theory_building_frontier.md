# Where Is the Frontier When the Work Gets Easier?

Draft, 2026-10-16.

## The Question

If proof, computation, modeling and reporting all get easier with AI, where is
the frontier? Maybe on the theory building side: understanding why a model
behaves the way it does, and what it tells us about the biology.

## Where the Idea Comes From

[Grant Sanderson's video on 3Blue1Brown](../../notes/podcasts/never-been-a-better-time-to-go-into-math.qmd)
asks this about mathematics. It works through a proof machine, a conjecture
machine and a theory machine. Even if AI gets very good at proofs, there are
large opportunities in theory building.

Terence Tao's [Mathematics in the age of AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16753)
makes a related argument: a proof that no human can properly explain should be
viewed as incomplete. The
[post on publishing explanations](../posts/2026-10-15-explaining-the-work-as-part-of-publishing/index.qmd)
carried that over to pharmacometrics.

## The Work I Have Valued Most

Looking back at my own published work, the parts I value most gave theory and
insight.

- **AFIR, AFTIR and SSIM for biologics.** Metrics that say how much target a
  drug engages, and what drives it.
- **CAR-T models.** Relatively simple models of a complex therapy.
- **CML modeling.** Theory and insight into the disease and its treatment.

## Where I Want to Take It

The same kind of question is open in the current work: TCE models, the PK and
B-cell models, and the sources of variability in how parameters affect response.

## Why It Is Exciting

If the human insight is what is exciting, then AI is a thinking tool. It can
crunch away at the proofs, the simulations and the reports, and help us get to
the insight faster. That might be one big direction for me.
